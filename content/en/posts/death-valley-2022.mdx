## Overview

This was a three-day Death Valley trip with a very clear theme:

1. Real desert, real wind.
2. Camping failed fast, so we moved to a hotel.
3. We still filled the next two days with the classic stops.

## Day 1 | Camping Start, Then The Wind Turned Up

The first impression at the campsite was simple: this is very much a desert. The landscape felt harsh, dry, and exposed, and the campground was basic.

It looked manageable during the day. At night the wind came in hard. By the next morning the tent was damaged, and the plan had to be rewritten immediately.

## Day 2 | Emergency Hotel Switch

The second day became a survival-first day. A nearby hotel had one small double room left, so we took it. Seven people from two families squeezed into the room, but at least we could wash off the dust and reset.

The conclusion was immediate: **next time in Death Valley, a hotel is the safer plan.**

After settling in, we still went back out and covered nearby viewpoints.

## Takeaways

1. Death Valley camping is wilder than it looks on paper, and wind should not be underestimated.
2. With older family members or kids, a hotel saves a lot of stress.
3. Even with the early mess, we covered the main sights and the trip still felt worth it.
