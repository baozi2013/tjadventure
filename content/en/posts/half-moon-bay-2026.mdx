This Half Moon Bay outing was an easy July half-day on the coast: first watching seals and seabirds from a bluff, then stepping into a coastal cypress grove, and finally waiting for the fog to open over purple and white seaside flowers.

## Overview

The album timestamps put the main photo window around **1:12 PM to 5:48 PM (PDT)**. This was not a checklist-style route; it was more about slowly collecting a few coastal scenes around Half Moon Bay: wildlife, cypress trees, fog, flowers, and ridgelines.

- Route: seal overlook -> short cypress grove walk -> coastal flower photos
- Pace: half-day drive plus light walking, with low physical effort
- Weather: fog and wind are real on the coast, but the sun can warm things quickly
- Best for: an easy ocean stop, photos, and a simple walk

![Seals and seabirds on a Half Moon Bay area beach](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day1-01.jpg)

## Day 1 | Seal Overlook And Bluff Wind

The first memorable stretch was the view down onto the beach. Seals were resting near the surf line, with a large group of seabirds nearby; the scene looked quiet from above, though the wind and ocean noise were probably strong in person.

![Seals resting near the surf line](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day1-02.jpg)

This kind of stop is best enjoyed from the bluff, without trying to get close to the animals. The photos work better with some distance anyway: surf, sand, and cliff edge all in the same frame feel very Northern California.

## Day 1 | A Short Walk Through Cypress Trees

After the ocean overlook, the route shifted into a cypress grove. The mood changed completely: dry ground, dense trunks, and branches reaching sideways, giving the path a natural tunnel feeling.

![Cypress grove path near the Half Moon Bay coast](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day1-06.jpg)

This section does not need to be long. Its value is the change of rhythm: open ocean first, fog and flowers later, and this cypress grove in between so the whole half-day is more than just one coastal view.

![Gray ocean and low clouds](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day1-07.jpg)

## Day 1 | Coastal Flowers After The Fog Opened

The most photogenic part came later in the afternoon, in the purple and white coastal flowers. The early frames still had low cloud over the water, but the ridgeline and blue sky gradually opened up, layering purple agapanthus, white blooms, ocean, and hills together.

![Purple and white coastal flowers under low cloud](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day2-02.jpg)

The trick here was not finding one standard photo spot, but changing direction with the fog. Facing the ocean gave softer frames; facing the hills made the scene cleaner and brighter. When the fog had just opened, the sky and ridge colors felt gentler than harsh midday light.

![Flowers, ridgeline, and lifting fog](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day2-04.jpg)

![Purple agapanthus with coastal hills](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day2-05.jpg)

The easiest way to photograph the flower field is to keep the person near the edge or on existing paths, using the flowers as foreground instead of stepping deep into them. The photo gets more depth, and the plants are better protected.

![Portrait among coastal flowers](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day2-08.jpg)

![Coastal flowers in fog](/trips/half-moon-bay-2026/day2-09.jpg)

## Logistics And Safety

This half-day route works best by car. The coastal stops around Half Moon Bay are not far apart, but parking can be unpredictable on weekends or around holidays, so it is worth taking a good spot when one appears.

- Layers: bring a jacket even in July; fog, wind, and sun can trade places quickly.
- Shoes: bluff edges, the cypress grove, and flower-field edges are better with comfortable walking shoes.
- Wildlife: keep distance from seals and seabirds, and stay at safe viewpoints.
- Photos: use the flowers as foreground, but avoid stepping into planted or pathless areas.
- Timing: late afternoon can be especially good if the fog starts lifting.

The practical rule is simple: bring layers, walk carefully near bluff edges, keep distance from wildlife, and let the fog be part of the plan instead of fighting it.

## Takeaways

1. Half Moon Bay works well as an easy half-day outing; a few coastal stops are enough.  
2. The seals, seabirds, and surf line made a natural opening, best watched quietly from a distance.  
3. The cypress grove added a different rhythm, even with only a short walk.  
4. The flower field depended on changing weather; the transition between fog and blue sky was the best photo window.  
5. Even in July, do not dress only for summer; a wind layer and comfortable shoes matter more than expected.  

My favorite frame from this trip has purple flowers, white flowers, gray ocean fog, and a sudden patch of blue sky all at once. That is often where Half Moon Bay looks best: in the middle of changing weather.
