## Overview

This was a day trip to **The Huntington Library, Art Museum, and Botanical Gardens**, just outside Los Angeles. The grounds are huge — the Rose Garden, Japanese Garden, Liu Fang Yuan (Chinese Garden), and the bamboo path each have a completely different character, so the day felt packed but rewarding.

We started with the art collections inside the mansion, moved through the Rose Garden, the lily ponds, and the Japanese Garden, then saved the best part — Liu Fang Yuan — for a slow afternoon walk, capping the day off with a stop at the gift shop.

## The Mansion And Gardens | From Art Collections To Roses And Lily Ponds

Right inside the mansion, the European-style hall has massive tapestries on the walls, a crystal chandelier, and antique furniture — full of history and worth taking your time with.

![Art collection hall inside the mansion](/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-1.jpg)

The Rose Garden was in full bloom, with orange-red roses climbing the trellis — leaning in for a smell was the must-do moment here.
![Enjoying the Rose Garden](/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-2.jpg)

By the lily pond, we ran into a turtle sunning itself while the water lilies were in full bloom, perfect for an overhead shot.
![Turtle and water lilies at the lily pond](/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-3.jpg)

## Japanese Garden | Pines, Koi Pond, And A Zen Courtyard

The old pine in the Japanese Garden has a very Zen shape, and the koi pond right beneath it made for a peaceful scene with great light.
![Pine and koi pond in the Japanese Garden](/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-4.jpg)

Deeper in the garden, there's a white-walled courtyard where a shaped pine leans out over the wall — a very clean, striking frame.
![Zen courtyard in the Japanese Garden](/optimized/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-5.webp)

## Liu Fang Yuan | The Most Stunning Stop Of The Day

**Liu Fang Yuan**, the Chinese Garden, was the single most photogenic stop at the Huntington — the moon gate frames the pavilion and lake perfectly no matter how you shoot it.

![Moon gate at Liu Fang Yuan](/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-6.jpg)

The pavilion across the lake reflects beautifully on the water, and the willows and Lake Tai rock formations capture the feel of a classical Jiangnan garden really well.
![Full view of the lake at Liu Fang Yuan](/optimized/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-7.webp)

There was also a calligraphy activity set up in the garden, with brushes and ink stones on a table — a fun hands-on way to leave your mark.
![Calligraphy activity at Liu Fang Yuan](/optimized/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-8.webp)

Leaving Liu Fang Yuan, we passed through a bamboo path where tall stalks blocked out the sun overhead — refreshingly cool to walk through.
![Bamboo path](/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-9.jpg)

## Gift Shop Finale

After a full day of gardens, we ended with a stop at the gift shop — the giant plush toys were impossible to resist and made for a relaxed way to close out the day.

![Giant plush toy at the gift shop](/trips/huntington-library-gardens-2026/day1-10.jpg)

## Takeaways

1. The Huntington grounds are huge — plan your route in advance and treat the Rose Garden, Japanese Garden, and Liu Fang Yuan as the three must-see stops.
2. Liu Fang Yuan is the most visually stunning stop overall; afternoon light works best for photos there.
3. Hands-on activities like the calligraphy table are worth setting aside time for — they leave a stronger memory than photos alone.
4. On hot days, wear sunscreen and bring water; alternating between the shaded Japanese Garden and the bamboo path helps keep the walk comfortable.
