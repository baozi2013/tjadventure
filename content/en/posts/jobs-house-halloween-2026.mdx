On Halloween night we went trick-or-treating at the Jobs family home in Palo Alto. In one line: **both the candy and the decorations go all out**. The whole yard was turned into a futuristic robot factory called **Robo Industries Inc.**, with live performers, fog and stage lighting, and the candy bag we left with had a full 180 g chocolate bar in it.

## Trip Overview

- When: the evening of October 31. Our photos run from **7:24 to 8:13 pm**, and walking through the display took about fifteen minutes.
- Where: the Old Palo Alto neighborhood, a regular residential street you walk in and out of.
- Theme: Robo Industries Inc., a robot factory, plus an Innovation Lab and a **REBOOT: 2076** product-launch stage.

## Robo Industries: A Robot Factory

The first thing you see is the facade of a whole "factory building": a big **ROBO INDUSTRIES INC** sign, brick chimneys, glass windows, and a glowing antenna tower on top. Robots stand on the upstairs balcony and the trees are lit red, so the mood is set right away.

## The Smoking Capsule

Further in is a huge cylindrical capsule with rings of red lights and fog pouring out of the top. An "engineer" in an orange suit works the control panel beside it, as if a new robot is about to be "booted up". This was the most crowded spot, with everyone waiting for the fog-and-light moment to take a photo.

## The Candy: Truly Generous

When we opened the candy bag at home, "generous" turned out to be an understatement:

- The bag itself is a burlap drawstring bag printed with a castle, bats and a witch, good enough to reuse.
- A full **Tony's Chocolonely caramel sea salt milk chocolate bar (180 g)**, wrapped in a custom Happy Halloween sleeve.
- Two small packs of **Jelly Belly** assorted flavors.
- A mango **Hi-Chew** stick.
- Two **Dum Dums** lollipops and a small hard candy.

Most houses hand out one or two small candies. This was a whole bag, and the chocolate bar alone will last the kids several days.

## Practical Tips

- Crowds: this house is well known for its display, and it gets busy after dark. Go early if you want clearer photos.
- Parking: it's a residential neighborhood. Park a bit further away and walk in, and don't block neighbors' driveways.
- Photos: the yard is dark and the lighting changes quickly, so phone night mode blurs easily. Hold steady and take several shots, or just shoot video.
- Etiquette: it's a private home. Follow the flow of people, and stay within the display.

## Takeaways

- The display tells a full story: factory building, lab, capsule, then the REBOOT: 2076 launch. Walking through feels like a small immersive show.
- The live performers make the yard come alive, and kids find it far more fun than static pumpkins and skeletons.
- The candy really is generous: a burlap bag plus a full 180 g Tony's Chocolonely bar made this the best stop of the night.
- Crowds are a given. Go early, take your time and take plenty of photos, and it's a much better experience.
