On an afternoon in the second weekend of November we walked around downtown Los Altos to photograph the fall colors. Autumn in the Bay Area is subtle, but Los Altos was at its best: **the trees along Main Street had turned red and orange, and the lampposts already had Christmas bows**. We finished on a whole street of golden ginkgos in a nearby neighborhood.

## Trip Overview

- When: the afternoon of November 8, about an hour and a half, all on foot.
- Route: Main Street and the blocks around it → two murals and a corner flower planter → a ginkgo-lined street in a nearby neighborhood.
- Highlights: red-orange street trees, early Christmas bows, two photogenic murals, golden ginkgos, and a black cat passing by.

## Main Street: A Street Of Red

Main Street is red and orange from end to end. The street trees line both sides, their crowns bright against the blue sky, and the red curbs are covered in fallen leaves. The corner lampposts are already wrapped in green garland with big red bows, so fall and Christmas meet on the same street.

## Side Streets: Red Trees And A Statue

Around the corner, two big red trees stand side by side in front of a stone building, a pinker red than on Main Street. Further on is a bronze statue, with a large monarch butterfly painted on the dark blue building behind it. A white cottage has a wooden bench by its door in full sun, a good spot to sit for a minute and take a few photos.

## Two Murals

Downtown has two walls made for photos. One is a colorful illustrated **LOS ALTOS** mural in blue, yellow and green, with an old cinema marquee reading "The Last Picture Show / The Great American Cowboy". The other is a black-and-white trompe-l'oeil mural of a wooden wall, windows and a parked bicycle; standing in front of it feels like stepping into an old photo.

## The Corner Planter And Lamppost

A corner on Main Street was the most photogenic spot of the walk: a big orange tree, yellow mums in a stone planter, a crosswalk, a STOP sign and an old-style streetlight with a red bow, all in one frame. Look up and the crowns mix red and yellow against the sky; turn around and the red trees run down the whole street.

## The Ginkgo Street: Gold From End To End

After downtown we went to a ginkgo-lined street in a nearby neighborhood. Tall ginkgos fill both sides, at their yellowest, with a few red trees mixed in for strong contrast. The ground is covered in small fan-shaped leaves, and holding one over an eye or next to your face makes for fun photos.

Partway through, a black cat strolled across the middle of the street, a nice surprise to end on.

## Practical Tips

- Timing: fall color comes late in the Bay Area. Los Altos looks best around early to mid November, which is also when the ginkgos turn gold.
- Light: the crowns glow most in the slanting afternoon sun. Once the sun drops, residential streets fall into shade quickly.
- Parking: there are public lots around downtown. On residential streets, don't block driveways.
- Etiquette: the ginkgo street is a quiet neighborhood. Stay to the side of the road when taking photos and watch for cars.

## Takeaways

- No long drive needed: downtown Los Altos has a whole street of red trees, easy to walk in an hour and a half.
- The Christmas bows are already up, so you get fall color and holiday mood in the same shots.
- The two murals and the corner planter are the easiest photo spots.
- Look for a ginkgo street in the nearby neighborhoods; the gold feels completely different from downtown's red.
