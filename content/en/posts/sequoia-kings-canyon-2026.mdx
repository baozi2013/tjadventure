This was a weekend trip to **Sequoia + Kings Canyon National Parks**. Day 1 was giant trees and Moro Rock. Day 2 followed Kings Canyon Scenic Byway all the way to Road's End. We got trees, canyon, river, waterfalls, and many "standing next to a huge tree" photos. The Tesla handled two park days on a full charge without range anxiety, but Holiday Inn Visalia was a miss and goes on my personal blacklist.

## Overview

This route works well as a weekend version:

- Day 1: Sequoia National Park, entrance sign, Tunnel Rock, Tunnel Log, Giant Forest, General Sherman Tree, Moro Rock
- Day 2: Kings Canyon National Park, canyon road, Kings River, Grizzly Falls, Cedar Grove / Road's End
- Lodging: Visalia is convenient for supplies, but I would not repeat Holiday Inn Visalia after this stay
- Transportation: many mountain roads and curves; Tesla FSD saved a lot of driving energy on the long transfers

With only two days, I would keep the plan simple: one park per day. Sequoia is for giant trees and high viewpoints. Kings Canyon is for depth, river, waterfalls, and the scenery at the end of the road.

## Day 1 | Giant Trees, Tree Photos, And Moro Rock

We entered Sequoia and started with the park sign. It is the most standard "we were here" photo, but a national park weekend feels incomplete without it.

![Sequoia National Park entrance photo](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-01.jpg)

Next was Tunnel Rock. It may not be the most dramatic stop in the park, but it works well as the opener: one huge granite boulder over the old road, with people underneath becoming the scale reference.

![Tunnel Rock photo stop](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-02.jpg)

Tunnel Log is the fallen sequoia that cars can drive through. The sign says it fell in 1937, with a 21-foot base diameter and 275-foot length. It has more of a "drive-through landmark" feeling than Tunnel Rock.

![Tunnel Log drive-through photo](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-09.jpg)

The fun of the giant sequoia area is that people automatically become measuring sticks. In photos the trees can look like ordinary wood, but in person you look up and realize you are standing beside a breathing wall.

![Photo stop by a giant sequoia log](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-03.jpg)
![Giant sequoias near General Sherman Tree](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-04.jpg)

This day had a high density of tree photos: standing near hollows, roots, fences, and trunks. Giant sequoias are good for humility. They make people look small without trying.

![Giant tree photo stop](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-07.jpg)
![Tree hollow photo spot](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-08.jpg)

In the afternoon we climbed Moro Rock. Official descriptions call it a popular short trail, and the staircase is not long, but the elevation and grade still make you breathe. The payoff is direct: the Great Western Divide, valley, and distant snow all open from the viewpoint.

![Family photo at Moro Rock viewpoint](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-05.jpg)
![Snowy mountains and valley from Moro Rock](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day1-06.jpg)

## Day 2 | Canyon Road, Big River, Waterfall, And Road's End

The second day moved to Kings Canyon. It has a different character from Sequoia: Sequoia says "the trees are huge"; Kings Canyon says "the valley is deep." As the road goes in, the mountains, river, and curving highway gradually stretch the sense of space.

![Kings Canyon scenic road view](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day2-01.jpg)

The most noticeable sound on the road was the river. In late spring, Kings River had strong flow, and many pullouts brought us close to the water. With kids, keep feet controlled: the rocks and shallows look near, but the current is serious.

![Rushing Kings River](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day2-02.jpg)

Grizzly Falls is an easy roadside waterfall. After parking, it takes little walking to see the water drop. It is good for a short stop, restroom break, and photos, but do not climb the wet rocks. Official guidance also warns that the rocks beside the falls are dangerous.

![Roadside Grizzly Falls](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day2-03.jpg)
![Canyon light beside the waterfall](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day2-09.jpg)

Continuing toward Cedar Grove / Road's End brought my favorite scene of the trip: green meadow at the road's end, a boardwalk by the river, and a full wall of mountain behind it. It is not a single "wow" viewpoint as much as a quiet, complete valley that appears after driving to the end.

![Road's End boardwalk and valley](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day2-04.jpg)
![Green meadow and granite wall](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day2-05.jpg)
![Meadow and mountains at Road's End](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day2-06.jpg)

This area is perfect for slowing down. Kids can stay around safe paths and open ground, while adults look at mountains, watch the river, and keep taking family photos.

![Road's End path](/trips/sequoia-kings-canyon-2026/day2-07.jpg)

## Transportation, Charging, And Lodging

The Tesla performed well. Starting with a full charge, we spent one day in Sequoia and one in Kings Canyon without range anxiety. The bigger value was FSD: Bay Area round trip plus mountain transfers consume a lot of attention, and driver assistance reduced fatigue. My suggestions:

- Start with a full charge, then top up on the return or the second morning depending on route.
- Mountain roads and air conditioning affect consumption, so do not treat displayed range as absolute.
- Park signal and supplies are limited. Prepare navigation, offline maps, and Supercharger plans ahead.
- The Highway 180 section to Cedar Grove / Road's End has seasonal closures, so check [NPS road conditions](https://www.nps.gov/seki/planyourvisit/road-conditions.htm/index.htm) before a spring trip.

For entry fees, use an America the Beautiful annual pass if you have one. Otherwise, check the official [Sequoia & Kings Canyon fees](https://www.nps.gov/seki/planyourvisit/fees.htm) page before leaving, since fees and non-resident rules can change.

I do not recommend Holiday Inn Visalia after this stay. The location was convenient, but the experience was poor. If staying near Visalia again, I would compare newer reviews and more stable chains, or look toward Three Rivers / Fresno.

## Takeaways

1. Two parks in two days is possible, but keep it to "Sequoia one day, Kings Canyon one day."
2. Sequoia's giant trees and Moro Rock are great for first-time visitors and memorable photos.
3. Kings Canyon gets better the deeper you drive toward Road's End: river, waterfall, valley.
4. A full-charge Tesla plus reasonable charging plan worked fine, and FSD helped on the long weekend drive.
5. Holiday Inn Visalia was a miss, so lodging should be chosen by more than location.

My favorite part was not one tree or one waterfall. It was the moment at the end of the road, where the green meadow and mountains stood quietly together. For a weekend, that was more than enough.
