## Overview

This was a one-day **Stars and Stripes 5K** event. The schedule was compact: morning warm-up, then the course, then photos and recovery near the finish area.

The good thing about this type of event is the low barrier. It has race atmosphere, but still feels relaxed enough for families.

## Before The Race | Warm Up And Find The Rhythm

When we arrived, the site was already lively, with many families and kids making final preparations. Arriving early helps leave time for stretching and walking around before the course starts.

## On The Course | Keep A Steady Pace

Once on the course, we did not chase speed. The goal was to finish steadily. There were plenty of parent-child groups and stroller families, and the atmosphere felt friendly rather than stressful.

![Middle course 1](/trips/stars-and-strides-5k-2025/day1-5.jpg)

For family participation, pacing matters more than chasing a time. Run with your breathing and cadence, and the finish will feel much better.

Near the finish, the arch, cheers, and music made the event atmosphere feel full. Those final minutes were the most satisfying and the most worth photographing.

## Recovery | Leave A Little Time To Slow Down

After the run, we rested on the grass and let heart rates come down slowly. This buffer matters for how everyone feels the next day, especially when kids are involved.

![Post-race rest on the grass](/trips/stars-and-strides-5k-2025/day1-16.jpg)

## Takeaways

1. A family 5K is very approachable, with controlled effort and a good sense of ceremony.
2. Arrive early, warm up, and keep a steady pace instead of sprinting too soon.
3. The finish area is the best photo window, so leave a few minutes for it.
4. Hydration, walking, and stretching after the race make recovery much better.
